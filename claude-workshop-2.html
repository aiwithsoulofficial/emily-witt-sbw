02Tune the system prompt

Open your Claude Project. Click Edit project then Project instructions. We are going to add two things: what is still going wrong, and a cleaner guide on how to handle client stories.

The question: when Claude hands you something back, what makes you cringe first?

Common answers from nervous system coaches after their first week:

  • Too much passive voice - "it can feel like" instead of "you feel"
  • Ending on a question when a quiet statement would land harder
  • Hedging too much - "this might resonate" and "perhaps" everywhere
  • Paragraphs that start with "And" trying to sound conversational but feeling lazy
  • Clinical words sneaking back in - "dysregulation", "activation" used loosely

Whatever yours is, add it to the NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES block. One line. Specific. Like this:

Too vague

Do not be clinical.
Never use "dysregulation", "activation", "dorsal", "ventral", or "sympathetic" in client-facing content. Translate them: "your body going into alarm" instead of "sympathetic activation."

Too vague

Sound like me.
Never end a caption with a question. End with a statement that lands like a full stop. Examples of mine: "That is the work." / "She already knows." / "We are allowed to stop here."

Add this block to your system prompt now

Scroll to the bottom of your project instructions and add the block below. Fill in the bracketed parts yourself based on the first week.

Add to the bottom of your Project InstructionsVOICE CALIBRATION (updated [date]) These are the specific errors I noticed in the first week. Add to this list as we go. [Your specific issue 1 here - e.g. "Never hedge with 'perhaps', 'might', or 'it can feel like'. State things directly. If I am uncertain whether something applies, I will say so in my prompt, not in the output."] [Your specific issue 2 here - e.g. "Never start a sentence with 'And' for emphasis. It reads as trying too hard."] [Your specific issue 3 here - e.g. "Captions end on a statement. Not a question. Not a call to action. A landing."] WHEN I SHARE CLIENT MOMENTS If I share something a client said or a moment from a session, assume I want to protect her completely. Never use any detail that could identify her. Default to: no name, no profession, no location, no specific life circumstances. Keep only the emotional truth.
Once you have added this: test it. Paste a piece of content that went wrong last week and ask Claude to rewrite it. You will see the difference immediately.

03Live content stack

This is the one you will run every week. It takes a voice memo and produces everything you need to post for three to four days.

Pull up a voice memo from this week - anything you recorded walking, driving, or just sitting with a thought. It does not have to be polished. If you do not have one, describe a client moment from the past few days to Kelly now and she will help you build it.

Step one - get the transcript

On your phone, open the voice memo. If you use the iOS Voice Memos app, press the three-dot menu and tap Transcribe. Give it ninety seconds. Copy the full text.

Alternatively, paste the audio into Otter.ai - the free plan does five hours per month.

Step two - run the stack prompt

Open your Claude Project. Start a new conversation. Paste this:

The full stack prompt - replace the bracketed bitsI have a voice memo transcript below. I want you to turn it into a full content stack. Work through each format one at a time. Do not move to the next format until I give you a thumbs up on the current one. Format 1: Instagram caption. Hook that lands in one breath. Three to five short paragraphs of real substance. A quiet statement at the end - not a question. No call to action. Format 2: Three Instagram story slides. Each slide is one thought. Under twelve words per slide. Not a numbered list, not a sequence - three independent moments from the memo that each stand alone. Format 3: Short email to my list. Subject line that does not try too hard. First line earns the read. Body pulls one insight from the memo and deepens it slightly. Last line is a quiet invitation to reply, not to buy anything. Transcript: """ [paste your transcript here] """ Start with Format 1. Wait for my feedback before Format 2.
Work through it one format at a time. Give Claude a real edit note if something is off: "the third paragraph is trying too hard, strip it back" or "the ending is too soft, make it land harder." Keep the feedback specific and one thing at a time.

If the first draft is close but not quite right

Use this prompt. It is faster than trying to describe the problem:

Refinement promptRead the draft back to yourself as if you are me. Identify the one line that sounds the least like me and tell me what it is. Then rewrite just that line three different ways - slightly softer, as written, slightly more direct - and let me pick.

When you love a piece

Save it as a sample. In your project instructions, add a section called SAMPLE CONTENT I WOULD WRITE AGAIN and paste it in. This trains Claude on your real voice over time, not just a description of it.

04Canva connector

Turn the best line from today's content into a real graphic - a reel cover or a quote card - without leaving Claude.

Connect it once

  1. In Claude, click your profile picture (top right), then Settings.
  2. Click Connectors or Integrations in the left menu.
  3. Find Canva. Click Connect. Log in to your Canva account when prompted.
  4. Come back to your Project. Canva is now connected for all future conversations.

Create a reel cover now

Take the strongest single line from the content you just made. Copy it. Then paste this prompt:

Canva reel cover promptCreate a new Canva design for me. I want an Instagram reel cover - 1080 x 1920 pixels. Brand: Space Between Wellness Palette: dusty pink #E4B5C1, espresso #522C1F, cream #F5F1ED Fonts: Playfair Display for headings, Work Sans for body Headline text: "[paste your best line here]" Sub-text: "Space Between Wellness" Layout: text-forward. Clean. Plenty of breathing room. No stock images. No busy background. The text should feel like it was placed with intention, not scattered. Generate the design and show me a preview link.
If Canva comes back with something that needs adjusting, just say what you want changed: "make the headline larger", "switch to a cream background", "move the sub-text to the bottom". It edits live.

Create a quote card

A square (1080 x 1080) with one of the story slide lines:

Quote card promptCreate a square Canva design, 1080 x 1080px, for a static Instagram post. Brand: Space Between Wellness. Dusty pink, espresso, cream. Single line of text: "[paste story slide text here]" Small footer text: "@spacebetweenwellness" Minimal. Generous white space. The kind of thing someone screenshots and saves.

05Two-week content map

One topic, mapped into two weeks of posts so you never open Instagram wondering what to say.

Give Claude a topic you are sitting with right now. Something you said to a client this week, or something you keep wanting to post about but have not quite cracked yet.

Content map promptI want to build a two-week content map from a single topic. The topic is: [your topic here - e.g. "the difference between being fed up and being broken"]. Map it out like this: - Week 1, five posts: go from surface-level observation to something more real. Start where she already is. End where she did not expect to go. - Week 2, five posts: responses, deepening, a different angle on the same truth. For each post: the format (caption, reel, story series, email), the angle, and the first line only. Not the full draft. Just the angle and the hook. Then pick the two strongest posts from the map and ask me which one to write first.
Save the map. Paste it into a note on your phone or a doc in your Drive folder. When you wake up on a Monday with nothing to say, open it and pick one.

Building a content backlog

Once you have done this twice you will have four weeks of posts planned. After six weeks you will have a rotating backlog. The goal is that you are always choosing what to post from a short list, never starting from nothing.

To refill the backlog at any time:

Refill prompt - run monthlyHere are three topics I keep coming back to lately: 1. [topic] 2. [topic] 3. [topic] For each one, give me three post angles I have not used yet. One reel hook, one caption angle, one story series concept. Do not write the full content. Just the angle and first line. I will schedule a batching session to write them.

06The chaining technique

Most people stop after one prompt. This is where the real leverage is. Chaining means taking Claude's output and immediately feeding it to a different task in the same conversation.

You never have to start fresh. Everything builds on the last thing.

Chain 1

Caption to email

After Claude writes you a caption you like, say this:

"Take the core idea from that caption and expand it into a short email for my list. Keep the same emotional register. The email should feel like a longer, more private version of the same thought - not a repeat of it."

Chain 2

Email to subject lines

After the email is done:

"Give me five subject line options for that email. Range them: one very plain, one slightly intriguing, one that starts mid-sentence, one that is just a fragment, one that sounds like something I would text a friend."

Chain 3

Good output to system prompt improvement

When Claude writes something you would genuinely post without changing a word:

"That is exactly right. Read that output back and tell me what specific language choices made it work. Then add those as a note to the system prompt under VOICE CALIBRATION."

This is how the system improves itself. You are training Claude on your real outputs, not just a description of them.

07Prompts to keep forever

These go alongside the five from last session. Save them in a note or your Drive folder.

The voice check

Run this when something feels almost-rightRead this back as if you are me. Give me a score out of ten for how close it is to my voice. Then tell me the exact word or phrase that is the furthest from how I speak - and why. Rewrite just that part.

The hard question

Run this when you are stuck on a topicI want to write about [topic] but I keep going too surface-level or too clinical. Ask me three questions that would pull out the real thing I am trying to say - the version that is a bit more honest than what I usually post. Do not write the post yet. Just ask the questions.

The brevity edit

Run this on anything that feels longCut this by thirty percent. Keep every idea. Remove every word that is not doing work. Do not summarise - compress. Show me the cuts in a second version.

The reset

Run this if Claude starts drifting from your voiceRe-read your project instructions from the beginning. Then read the sample content I uploaded. Now rewrite the last output from scratch as if those are the only two documents you have ever seen.

The client moment

Turn a session insight into contentSomething happened in a session this week that I keep thinking about. I am going to describe it in general terms - no identifying details. I want you to help me find the piece of content hidden in it. Here is the moment: [describe it - what happened, what shifted, what she said, what you noticed] Give me three different angles I could write a post from. Do not write the posts yet. Just name the angles - what each one would be about at its core.

Session 3

Wire Claude into your application form workflow. When someone submits an application, Claude drafts a personalised reply in your voice before you even open your inbox. We also build your welcome sequence properly - not just draft it, but get it sent automatically from day one.
