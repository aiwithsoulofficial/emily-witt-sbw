How we see what's working - and what isn't.

First, why bother tracking anything?

Right now, your website is live and getting visitors - but it's a black box. You post on Instagram, someone taps the link in your bio, lands on your site... and then what? Did they read the whole page? Did they click the application button? Did they leave immediately? Without any tracking in place, there's no way to know.

Tracking gives you a feedback loop. It's the difference between guessing which content is working and actually knowing. That matters when you're deciding what to post next, whether to run ads, and where to put your energy.

Think of it like a nervous system - without signals, you can't respond. Tracking is the signal that tells you what your audience is actually doing, so you can respond with intention rather than reaction.

A note on the privacy question

This is worth addressing directly, because you're someone who thinks about ethics and you should feel clean about what's on your site.

The tools we're installing track actions, not people. They register things like: "someone viewed the 6-week programme page" or "someone clicked the apply button." They don't capture names, email addresses, or any information that identifies a specific person. The data is anonymous and aggregate.

The Meta Pixel (explained below) gets the most scrutiny because Meta is Meta - but the pixel itself is industry standard. Every business you've ever bought from uses one. We'll add a proper privacy policy page that discloses everything, and if it ever becomes relevant, we can add a cookie consent banner too.

The short version: this is basic business feedback, not surveillance. And you'll have full ownership and control of all of it.

Three tools we're setting up

Each one has a different job. Together they give you a complete picture.

Meta Pixel + Conversions API

Essential for ads

The Meta Pixel is a tiny piece of code that connects your website to your Meta Business account (the same account that runs your Instagram and Facebook). When someone visits your site after seeing your Instagram content, the pixel can see that connection - matching the visit to an action like filling in your application form.

This is what makes Meta ads actually work. Without it, you can run ads but you can't track whether they lead to enquiries, and you can't build retargeting audiences (i.e. show ads specifically to people who visited your site but didn't apply). We set this up now so it's ready when you decide to run ads - it also just starts collecting audience data in the background from day one, which is valuable.

The Conversions API is an upgrade to the pixel - it sends event data server-side (directly from the website's backend to Meta) rather than just browser-side, which means more reliable tracking even when people use ad blockers. Kelly sets both up at the same time.

Google Analytics 4 (GA4)

Essential for traffic

GA4 is Google's free analytics platform. It's the overall dashboard for your website - how many people visit, where they came from (Instagram, Google, direct), how long they stay, which pages they read, and which pages they leave from immediately.

If Meta Pixel tells you how your ads are performing, GA4 tells you everything else: is your organic Instagram driving traffic? Are people finding you on Google? Is the homepage holding attention or losing people in the first 10 seconds? It's the broadest view of your audience's behaviour.

Google Search Console

Free - must have

Search Console is not an analytics tool - it's Google's direct communication channel with you about your website. It shows you what search terms people type into Google before landing on your site, how often your pages appear in search results, how many people click through, and whether Google has any issues indexing (reading) your pages.

This one costs nothing and requires almost no ongoing attention. We set it up, submit your sitemap (the list of all your pages), and let Google do its thing. Over time it becomes one of the most useful tools you have for understanding how strangers find you organically.

TikTok Pixel

Optional - later

Only relevant when you start running TikTok ads. The setup is almost identical to the Meta Pixel process. We'll add it when the time comes - skip it for now.

What we'll track once it's live

Installing the base code automatically captures PageView (someone loaded a page) on every visit. Beyond that, Kelly will set up specific events - that's the word for a tracked action - so you can see meaningful behaviour, not just raw traffic.

Here's what's planned for v1:

Event name What it means Where it fires
PageView Someone loaded a page - automatic, no extra setup needed Every page
Lead Someone submitted the application form /apply.html (on form submit)
Lead Someone signed up for your newsletter Flodesk form (once wired up)
Contact Someone clicked a booking or enquiry button Any "book" or "apply" button
ViewContent Someone viewed the 6-week intensive page - used to build a retargeting audience of warm leads 6-week programme page

These event names (Lead, Contact, ViewContent) are Meta's standard vocabulary. Using them correctly means Meta's algorithm can learn who your most likely clients are and show your ads to similar people over time.

What you'll be able to do once this is set up

This is the part worth sitting with, because it's a genuine shift in how you can run your business.

  • Run Meta ads with real conversion tracking. Instead of guessing whether an ad campaign drove enquiries, you'll be able to see it directly. Cost per lead. Which creative performed. Which audience responded.
  • Build retargeting audiences. Anyone who visited your 6-week intensive page but didn't apply can be grouped into an audience. You can then run a specific ad just for them - a gentle nudge to the people who were already interested enough to look.
  • See which organic content actually drives action. Not just likes and reach - but whether the people who clicked through from a specific Instagram post went on to read your programme page or fill in your application.
  • Track your content's real-world impact. When you post a Reel and your Instagram insights say 400 people tapped the link in bio, you'll be able to see in GA4 exactly what those 400 people did next.
  • Know your funnel numbers. How many people visit the site in a month. How many view the 6-week programme page. How many click apply. How many complete the form. Once you know those numbers, you know exactly where to focus.

Privacy and ethics - the honest version

A few things worth knowing and being clear on with your audience:

What a privacy policy needs to say

Any website using Meta Pixel or Google Analytics needs a privacy policy page that discloses this. It doesn't need to be intimidating - it's a short page that says what data is collected, by whom, and why. This is a TODO for Kelly to write next. It will go at /privacy.html with a link in the footer.

Australian Privacy Act

For a Hobart-based service business with an Australian audience, the Australian Privacy Act 1988 is the main framework to be aware of. The tools we're using are standard and compliant. The privacy policy page covers your obligations.

GDPR (EU visitors)

If you had significant European website visitors, you'd need a cookie consent banner - the pop-up that asks permission before tracking runs. For a locally focused practice like yours, this is low priority. If you ever run international campaigns, we can add a lightweight consent tool at that point.
