Brand Essence

Brand Name

Your personal brand is Emily Witt. Space Between Wellness is your method and framework - the intellectual property that sits within your personal brand. Your audience connects with you first, then discovers the method.

Positioning Statement

Emily Witt helps high-achieving women who built the life they were told to want - and lost themselves inside it. Through The Space Between Method, grounded in psychological science and nervous system awareness, she guides women from autopilot to alignment. No quick fixes. No toxic positivity. Just honest, evidence-informed guidance woven with spiritual wisdom.

Typography

Three typefaces that each have a job - one feels, one grounds, one communicates.

Script Typeface
Space Between Wellness
The pathway back to you
Use for: Brand name accent, emotional taglines, pull quote highlights, hero flourishes
Weight: Regular (400) only - the brush does the work
Why: Handwritten warmth. Feels personal, human, like a note from a friend.
Rule: Never for body copy. One brush element per section maximum.
Heading Typeface
Playfair Display
Grounded in science. Woven with spirit.
Use for: Headlines, section titles, sub-headings, pull quotes
Weights: Regular (400), Medium (500), Semibold (600), Bold (700)
Why: High contrast serif with editorial authority. Pairs beautifully with the brush script.
Note: Use italic for emotional emphasis. Italic Playfair is stunning for quotes and intimacy.
Body Typeface
Work Sans - clean, warm, highly readable.
When everything feels heavy, clarity is an act of kindness.
Use for: Body copy, navigation, buttons, captions, forms
Weights: Light (300), Regular (400), Medium (500), Semibold (600)
Why: The clear, quiet one that lets the other two shine. Easy to read at 9pm on a phone.
Note: Body text at 15-16px minimum. Her audience is reading on phones - don't make her squint.

06

Visual Identity

How the brand looks and feels across every surface.

Photography Direction

  • Australian landscape - outback, golden hour, dawn light, red earth, native flora
  • Warm, natural tones - never over-saturated or cold-filtered
  • Women in nature - grounded, present, real (not posed wellness stock)
  • Texture - earth, stone, water, bark, sand, linen
  • Space and breath - compositions with room to exhale
  • Soft focus and natural light over studio lighting

Content Style

  • Video content = voiceover over lifestyle footage (never talking head)
  • Emily captured in her practices - reformer, sound baths, walking in nature
  • Text overlays use Alex Brush in Desert Blush or Warm Charcoal
  • Social posts: earthy warmth, large readable text, ample white space

Design Principles

PrincipleApplication
Space is sacredGenerous padding and margins. Let the content breathe.
Warmth over polishSlightly imperfect over sterile perfection. The brand should feel like a warm room.
Photo zone / text zoneImages and text never compete for the same space.
Texture over flatSubtle gradients, natural textures. Flat colour = Canva energy.
Mobile firstShe's reading on her phone at 9pm. Everything designed for thumb-scroll.

The Woman We Speak To

Not a target demographic. A real human with a real feeling.

She's the woman who did everything right - the degree, the career, the house, the relationship. She ticked every box her mother modelled and society rewarded. On paper, she's thriving.

But she's exhausted in a way that sleep doesn't fix. She's disconnected from herself, running on autopilot, building something that doesn't feel like hers. She's been told to hustle harder, be grateful, set boundaries. None of it touched the real thing.

She doesn't think she's burned out. She's just... fed up. Ready to stop performing. Ready to feel something real again.

She found Emily because something in the words felt different. Not another coach promising transformation. Someone who actually gets it. Someone who made her nervous system settle before she even understood why.

"Too many women are running towards what they think success looks like - based on unmet needs, limiting beliefs, and what they've seen other people do. I want them to stop and ask: is this even mine?"

Emily Witt
