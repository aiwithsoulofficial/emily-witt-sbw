Phase 0 - The Foundation Week (Week 1, pre-launch)

Before you post a single thing

What needs to be in place

This is not busywork. If your bio is unclear or your highlights are a mess, every piece of content you post sends people to a profile that does not convert. One week of setup means 60 days of content actually landing somewhere.

Instagram bio - lock it this week

Your bio has one job: make the right person immediately feel seen and know what to do next. Here is the structure to work with:

Bio formula

Line 1: Who you help, in their language (not your job title)

Line 2: What you do / the method (one short phrase)

Line 3: Your differentiator (the science + spirit line)

Line 4: Where to go next (the action)

Draft option: "For women who did everything right and still feel fed up / Nervous system coaching grounded in psychological science / Hobart, Tasmania + online / Take the free quiz (link in bio)"

Highlights to create or rename

Five covers, five clear functions. Emily already has reel cover templates built - use those to create matching highlight covers in the same palette.

  • Start here - your intro reel, what you do, who it is for
  • The 4 states - one story per nervous system state (build this as you go)
  • The work - process, session snapshots, behind-application stories
  • Real stories - anonymised client wins and testimonials
  • Sound bath - your in-person Hobart offering, what it feels like in the room (secondary to 1:1)

Pinned posts - what goes up before Day 1

You want three pinned posts sitting at the top of your grid before the campaign starts. These do the heavy lifting for anyone who finds you mid-campaign and scrolls back to understand who you are.

  1. Your intro / "this is what I actually do" reel (this becomes Day 2 content if you have not filmed it yet)
  2. Your most resonant quote graphic from the 16 photos - something with "fed up is different to burnt out" or "the fire didn't go out" as overlay text
  3. The nervous system states explainer - a simple carousel or text post naming Activation, Over Giving, Shut Down, Regulated with one line each

The 30-post audit

Go back through your last 30 posts on Instagram. For each one, ask two questions: does this show what I actually do, and does it sound like me at my most grounded? Anything that feels like a different era, a different voice, or a different version of what you are offering - archive it. You are not deleting it, just moving it out of the way. The goal is that someone landing on your profile for the first time sees a coherent, confident practitioner from post one.

What to keep: anything that uses your nervous system language, shows the sound bath in its full beauty, or captures a genuine moment. What to archive: anything written in hustle-culture language, anything that mentions burnout, any post that feels like you were performing confidence rather than having it.

TikTok account - set up this week if you have not already

Username: @spacebetweenwellness (or closest available). Bio mirrors your Instagram bio. Link goes to the website. You will not post here until Week 5 at the earliest - but have it ready so you are not scrambling later.

LinkedIn - a note before you start

Your LinkedIn profile needs one update before Week 4 when you post there: your headline should name nervous system coaching, not just "wellness practitioner." Something like "Nervous System Coach for Women in Leadership | Psychological Science | Space Between Wellness" gives the algorithm and the reader enough to know they are in the right place.

Phase 1 - Days 1-14: Establish Presence (Weeks 2-3)

Phase 1 theme

"I'm here. And this is what I actually do."

The goal this phase is simple: make the right people feel immediately seen and give them a reason to follow. No selling. No CTA beyond "follow for more of this." You are planting the flag and letting the content do the positioning work for you.

Cadence

  • Monday to Friday: 1 reel or carousel per day
  • Daily: 1-3 stories (behind-the-scenes, polls, question stickers)
  • Sunday: 1 carousel (this is your quieter, more reflective post - good for personal story or framework content)

Pillar pattern - weeks 2 and 3

Each week rotates through four content pillars. Here is the split and the pattern for each.

Education (2x per week) - hook + reframe + name the thing

Hook: Something she has been thinking but has not heard named out loud.

Body: One piece of nervous system science or a framework reframe. Keep it to one idea. Three sentences max.

CTA: "Save this. Share it with someone who needs to hear it." No link needed this phase.

Personal story (1x per week) - your journey, not your credentials

Hook: Start mid-story, not at the beginning. Drop us in at the moment of tension.

Body: One specific moment. What you felt, what shifted, what you understand now that you did not then.

CTA: "Tell me if this lands for you." Invite conversation, not clicks.

Client moment (1x per week) - let her story do the work

Hook: A direct quote (anonymised) or a moment from a session - the shift, the realisation, the thing she said on her way out the door.

Body: Brief context. What was happening in the nervous system. What changed.

CTA: "She is not the only one. This is what the work looks like." No application link yet.

Behind the scenes (1x per week) - let them in

Hook: Something quiet and real. A stillness shot from the studio. The sound bath setup. A walk by the water before a session day.

Body: One or two lines of voiceover about what this space holds or what this practice means to you.

CTA: "This is where the work happens." No action required.

Phase 2 - Days 15-30: Build Trust (Weeks 4-5)

Phase 2 theme

"This is the work. Here is what it looks like."

You have shown up consistently for two weeks. Your audience is warmer. Now you go one layer deeper into the framework, introduce the four states as a mini-series, and make your first soft ask - the quiz. Still no direct session pitch. That comes in Phase 3.

The 4 States mini-series

One reel per week for four weeks (runs across Phases 2 and 3). Name each state, describe what it feels like in the body, remove the shame from it, and leave the door open. These four pieces of content will become some of your most-saved and most-shared. They are the backbone of your intellectual property on social.

  • Week 4: Activation - "driven, restless, always on. Rest feels like failure."
  • Week 5: Over Giving - "agreeable on the outside, screaming on the inside." This is Script 2 - film it or voiceover it over a walk.
  • Week 6: Shut Down - "foggy, flat, going through the motions. Not broken. Just depleted."
  • Week 7: Regulated - "this is the state where actual choices get made. Here is what it feels like."

First soft CTA - end of Week 4

On Day 26 or 27 (Thursday or Friday of Week 4), for the first time, direct people to the quiz in your bio. Caption: "If you read that and felt something, the quiz in my bio will tell you which state you have been living in. It takes two minutes and it might give you the clearest language you have had for this in a while."

LinkedIn starts in Week 4

One post per week, Thursday. Your LinkedIn audience is executives and leaders - they respond to evidence-informed frameworks, not emotional hooks alone. Lead with the science, follow with the human truth. Target 600-900 words. No lists of dot points - write in paragraphs. Sign off with a question to the comments.

Week 4 LinkedIn topic: "What your team's presenteeism is actually telling you" - introduce the Activation state through a workplace lens. This is the post that seeds the corporate offering without pitching it.

First testimonial reel - Week 5

Use the sound bath testimonial already in the welcome email sequence: "It was like something drew me toward you, knowing I was in for a bad day. It was truly something special - like a reset and rest my body truly needed." Film a voiceover reel with sound bath footage as the visual. Let the quote breathe. Do not add explanation - the quote is the content.

Phase 3 - Days 31-45: Convert to Action (Weeks 6-7)

Phase 3 theme

"If you are ready for a doorway in."

This is where you introduce the Clarity and Capacity Reset. Two reels position the offer. The rest of the content does not change - you are still running all four pillars. The offer sits inside the content rhythm, not on top of it. If she has been watching you for five weeks, the offer will feel like a natural next step, not a sales pitch.

Offer positioning - two reels, different angles

Reel 1 (Day 32): "What actually happens in a Clarity and Capacity Reset." Walk through the four things that happen inside the 90 minutes. Use Script 4 energy ("you ticked every box") as the opening context, then pivot to: here is what we do about it. Voiceover over the studio or sound bath visual is fine here. Face-to-camera if you are ready.

Reel 2 (Day 39): A client moment reel - anonymised, specific. "She came in running on Activation. She left knowing exactly which pattern was driving it, and with two tools she could use that afternoon." No before-and-after language. Just the reality of what shifts in 90 minutes of real work.

Soft urgency play

Do not manufacture urgency. Use what is real: you are solo, you are in Hobart, and you have a finite number of 1:1 slots available each month. From Day 38 onwards, your story CTAs can include: "I have four 1:1 slots available this month. If you have been thinking about it, the application is in my bio." That is the whole sentence. No countdown timers, no "last chance," no artificial scarcity.

Stories - the Behind the Application series (Days 38-45)

Three to five stories across this period showing what the application process actually looks like. Not a tutorial - a normalisation. "The application has six questions. They are not hard questions but they are honest ones. Most people tell me filling it out gives them clarity before we have even spoken."

TikTok - starts in Week 5

From Day 35, start repurposing your strongest three Instagram reels to TikTok. Same video, same caption, same hashtags. The extra time cost is posting. Aim for three times per week on TikTok maximum. If it starts to feel like work, drop to twice. Your nervous system awareness is the product - do not run your own in deficit to prove a point.

LinkedIn - Weeks 6 and 7

  • Week 6: "Why 'resilience training' is the wrong frame for high-performing teams" - introduce Over Giving through an organisational lens. No pitch. End with a question.
  • Week 7: Your personal story - depression at 12, the generational pattern, what you built from it. This is the post that converts LinkedIn followers into people who trust you enough to apply. Write it as a narrative, not a listicle. 800-1000 words. Sign off "Em x."

Phase 4 - Days 46-60: Optimise and Amplify (Week 8+)

Phase 4 theme

Read the room. Double down on what worked.

By Day 46 you have six weeks of data. Look at your top three reels by saves and profile visits (not likes - those are vanity, saves and visits are signals of actual intent). What are they about? That is your content priority for the next 60 days. Everything else informs but does not override.

What to do with your strongest content

  • Top reel by saves - pin it if it is not already pinned. Turn it into a carousel (pull the 3-4 key lines, make each one a slide). Boost it with $100 in Meta ads (see the Meta Ad plan below).
  • Top reel by profile visits - this is your acquisition content. It is making people curious enough to find out more. Turn it into a TikTok stitch or duet prompt if the topic allows.
  • Top carousel by shares - this one is being sent to people. It is your most relatable, most "this is my friend" content. Write a LinkedIn long-form version of the same idea.

LinkedIn long-form article - Week 8

Write a proper LinkedIn article using the Space Between Method as the framework. Title suggestion: "The Four Nervous System States That Are Running Your Leadership Team (And What To Do About Each One)." Structure: brief intro on nervous system science, one section per state, one practical application per state for leaders. 1200-1500 words. This is the article you send to corporate contacts and use as the basis for any speaking pitches.

Email nurture goes live - Week 8

The four-email welcome sequence is already written and ready in /welcome-emails.html. Week 8 is when you wire it into Flodesk and attach it to the bio link opt-in. From this point, anyone who clicks the quiz or signs up to your list enters the sequence automatically. Social drives people to the list. The list converts them to applications.

The First Meta Ad Campaign (Days 45-60)

What to boost and when

Wait until you have six weeks of organic data before spending a cent on ads. By Day 45 you will know which reel is your strongest by saves and profile visits. That is the one you boost. Do not guess. Let the algorithm show you first.

ExcludePeople who have already visited your website (retargeting is a later-stage play)

What to measure

Cost per link click Target under $2.50 AUD. If it is above $4, pause the ad and check whether the creative matches the audience or whether the landing page has a friction issue.
Application form submissions The actual conversion metric. If you spend $150 and get 2-3 qualified applications, that is a strong result for a first campaign. One converted session ($200) already covers the ad spend.
Video view rate For reel ads: what percentage watch past 3 seconds and past 15 seconds? If 3-second rate is under 40%, the hook is not working. Recut the opening 2 seconds.
Audience signal After 7 days, Meta will show you which interest categories are converting at the lowest cost. Let that reshape your Phase 5 targeting.

Do not run ads to a profile that is under 60 posts deep. The ad drives people to your profile and your link - if the profile does not convert them on arrival, you are paying for traffic that immediately leaves. Ads only go live in Week 8, after six weeks of content has built the container they are landing in.

Weekly Operations Plan

Everything below assumes you are working solo, from your phone or laptop, and you want to protect your own nervous system regulation while running a content schedule. This structure is designed to keep the time cost under 2 hours on heavy days and 20 minutes on light ones.

Sunday (1 hr) Review the week ahead in this document. Choose which hook you will use for each day. Open Notes on your phone and draft your five captions in one sitting. Schedule or pre-write them in Meta Business Suite or Later. Batch script the Sunday carousel for the following week while you're in creative mode.
Monday (1.5 hrs) Film day. Set up your filming spot (window light, clear background, phone on tripod or propped stable). Film three to five takes of each reel you need for the week. Choose your best take in playback. Open Canva or CapCut and drop your reel cover from the pre-built template set. Export and post Monday's reel. Schedule Tuesday's reel if you have it ready.
Tue - Fri (20 min/day) Post the day's reel or carousel (already edited from Monday). Add 2-3 stories - one can be a re-share of yesterday's post, one can be a question sticker or poll related to the week's theme. Reply to comments and DMs within the day. This is not optional - replies from early followers are some of your warmest future clients.
Saturday (optional, 30 min) TikTok repurposing only - from Week 5 onwards. Download your top three reels from Instagram (save from your own profile), re-upload to TikTok with the same caption. No re-editing required. This is a bonus, not a requirement. If your energy is low, rest. Your content will keep running without Saturday.
LinkedIn days (Thu, 45 min) From Week 4 only. Open the LinkedIn post draft you wrote on Sunday. Read it once, adjust the tone to be slightly more formal than your Instagram voice. Add a closing question. Post. Reply to any comments within 24 hours - LinkedIn rewards early engagement.

One practical note: if you batch-film on Monday and the footage is off - bad light, you were in Activation and it reads in your body - do not push through and use bad footage. Metrics That Actually Matter

You are a solopreneur with one offer, a finite number of monthly slots, and zero interest in chasing vanity numbers. Here is what to track, what it means, and what to do with the information.

Saves (priority one) Someone saving a post means they thought "I want to come back to this." It is the strongest single signal of resonance on Instagram. Check weekly: which post this week had the most saves? Make more content on that theme.
Profile visits Someone watching a reel and then going to your profile to find out who you are. This is the moment between "interested" and "considering." Track it per reel to know which content is driving discovery.
Link clicks from bio The number of people clicking through to your website, quiz, or application. This is your awareness-to-consideration metric. If profile visits are high but link clicks are low, the bio is not converting. Review it.
Application form submissions The actual conversion. Not traffic to the apply page - completed submissions. This is the number that matters. Track weekly in your GHL dashboard. Target: 2-4 qualified applications per week by the end of Phase 3.
Story replies (DM quality) When someone replies to a story, they are having a private conversation with you. These are warm leads. Every story reply deserves a genuine, personal response - not a template. Keep a note of anyone who engages here more than once. They are close to ready.
Reach (secondary) How many unique accounts your content reached. Useful for understanding growth trend, not for judging individual posts. A piece of content with 200 reach and 40 saves is more valuable than one with 2000 reach and 3 saves.

Likes are not a metric to optimise for. They feel good. They tell you almost nothing. A post with 12 likes and 8 saves from the right women is a better business result than a post with 300 likes from people who scrolled on.

The TikTok Question

Here is the honest call: TikTok is a bonus channel for Emily, not a priority. Here is why.

Her ideal client, the 34-42 high-achieving woman who is fed up and quietly carrying everything, does live on TikTok. The platform's algorithm is genuinely powerful for discovery content, and nervous system education performs exceptionally well there. In theory, TikTok should be a strong play.

In practice, repurposing is the only realistic TikTok strategy for a solo practitioner. Creating TikTok-native content (trending audio, direct-to-camera casual style, comment-reply format) is a different creative muscle than what works for Emily's brand on Instagram. The risk is that chasing TikTok native trends dilutes the brand voice she is working hard to establish on Instagram.

The recommendation: repurpose from Instagram from Week 5 onwards, maximum three times per week. Do not change the audio, do not add trending sounds, do not adapt the caption. Just re-upload exactly. If it picks up traction organically, great - then invest more. If after 30 days of repurposing it is not driving profile visits or follows, treat TikTok as a long-game presence play, not an active acquisition channel, and redirect that energy to deepening Instagram engagement.

The one scenario where TikTok becomes a serious investment: if one of Emily's repurposed reels gets picked up and crosses 10,000 views. At that point, create three TikTok-first versions of her strongest content themes (the fed-up reframe, the four states introduction, the science meets spirit positioning) and test whether the algorithm sustains reach at that level. Until then, do not optimise for a channel that has not yet proven itself for this audience.

The Riskiest Week in the Campaign

Week 6 (Days 31-37) is the riskiest week for Emily's energy. Here is why, and what to do about it.

Week 6 is where the campaign pivots from "showing up" to "asking." The first conversion reel goes live on Day 32. The application link starts appearing in captions. The soft urgency language ("four slots left this month") begins. For most practitioners, this is where the self-doubt arrives: Am I being too much? Is it too soon? What if nobody applies?

The practical risk is that Emily pulls back on content quality or frequency right at the moment the campaign needs to push through. The emotional risk is that she checks the application form obsessively and interprets zero immediate applications as evidence that the whole approach is wrong.

Two things to hold going into Week 6. First: the conversion content does not replace the education and personal content - it sits alongside it at a ratio of 2 conversion reels to 8 other pieces. Nobody is being sold to relentlessly. Second: the lag between someone seeing conversion content and someone applying is usually 7-14 days. Applications from Week 6 content are likely to arrive in Week 7 or 8. The calendar is working even when it does not look like it.

If Week 6 feels hard, the only adjustment worth making is to swap one face-to-camera conversion reel for a voiceover version over sound bath footage. Same words, same offer, lower exposure cost for Emily that day. The content still does the work.
