The real cost

The leaders who quietly check out before they resign are the ones you never see coming.

Burnout in leadership teams doesn't announce itself. It shows up as flattened decision-making, reduced capacity for complexity, and the kind of slow institutional knowledge drain that takes years to rebuild after someone leaves.

Under Australian WHS legislation, psychological hazards in the workplace are no longer a soft issue - they are a legal obligation. Managing vicarious trauma, chronic stress, and leadership team dysfunction is now part of your duty of care.

A wellness day doesn't address a dysregulated nervous system. A meditation app doesn't build the emotional capacity your leaders need under real pressure. The organisations that get this right invest differently.

Turnover + Replacement

Losing a senior leader costs an estimated 1.5 to 2x their annual salary in recruitment, onboarding, and lost productivity. The real cost is rarely calculated.

Decision Fatigue

Leaders operating in chronic dysregulation make reactive decisions. Risk tolerance distorts. Long-term thinking narrows. Teams feel the instability before anyone names it.

Lost Institutional Knowledge

The most exhausted people in your organisation often carry the most. When they leave - or quietly disengage - they take context, culture, and capability with them.

Culture as Performance

Wellbeing programs that sit outside actual work culture signal one thing clearly: this is compliance, not care. People notice. Engagement data eventually confirms it.

Scope of practice

Emily is not a registered psychologist or counsellor. Her qualifications are in psychological science and her organisational work is delivered as evidence-informed education, coaching, and consulting. She does not diagnose, assess, or treat clinical mental health conditions. This scope positions her work as a complement to, not a replacement for, clinical and EAP services - and it means organisations engage her with clarity on what they're investing in.

Eight years in the sector gives her something no certification does: she knows what your teams are actually carrying.

Emily spent eight years working in complex trauma and community services before building Space Between Wellness. She has trained and supported professionals in some of the most high-exposure roles in the sector - people whose nervous systems absorb the weight of other people's crises, day in and day out.

That background is not incidental to her corporate and organisational work. It is why she doesn't need to be briefed on vicarious trauma or sector stress in the abstract. She has navigated it alongside the people doing the work.

It is also why she builds programs the way she does: evidence-informed, grounded in nervous system science, and deeply respectful of the intelligence of the adults in the room. No condescension. No generic resilience tropes. Work that meets people where they actually are.
